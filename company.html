DTM · Dynamic Trajectory Memory

We build for people who have to get the work done.

DTM was founded from experience with the realities of administrative work: the unwritten knowledge, the handoffs, the exceptions, and the responsibility that remains with people long after a system has promised to make the work simpler.

Why we exist

Adding intelligence to a disorganized process doesn't make it better.

We believe AI can do far more useful work than it does today. But before we delegate a step to AI, we need to understand the work itself.

Our starting point is different

Delegating to AI should demand the same discipline as delegating to people.

We begin by asking what the work is for, who understands it, what can be delegated and what must remain a human decision. Good managers do not ask people to work without context, boundaries or accountability.

From there we look for the smallest repeatable operations that can safely carry the work forward. This is the thinking behind Janus OS, and the method we bring to consulting.

What we hold to

Protect attention. Preserve authority. Maintain understanding.

Protect attention. Human attention is scarce. People should spend it on decisions that need them, not on supervising every routine AI step.

Preserve authority. Responsibility doesn't disappear because an AI model carries out part of a process.

Maintain understanding. Work should leave enough context and evidence for people to explain, question and improve it.

We design for mistakes

Requiring perfection is a poor design decision.

Neither a person nor a model gets everything right on the first pass. A dependable system should account for that through bounded work, independent checks, clear exceptions and deliberate escalation.

We're testing the thinking on our own work

A practical place to learn what works and where it fails.

An early implementation of the Janus approach is helping DTM organize and check portions of its own software development. It shows us which controls work, where they fail, and when a person still needs to intervene.

We distinguish those internal observations from claims of broad customer performance.
