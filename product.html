Janus OS

An operating system for work done with AI.

Janus is being built as reusable infrastructure for delegating, coordinating and checking work: not a single chatbot, automation, or compliance checklist. People should not have to manage individual AI agents to benefit from their work.

The idea

Responsible delegation, applied to AI.

Janus takes the familiar principles of responsible human delegation and management and applies them to AI: clear instructions, assigned responsibilities, permitted actions, independent checks and escalation when judgment is needed.

Start with the work, not the model

A person who knows the job describes the outcome.

They describe the constraints and the important exceptions. The process can then be broken into bounded responsibilities instead of handing an entire job to one unconstrained prompt.

Give each step the right tool

Not every operation needs a powerful AI model.

Some steps belong to ordinary scripts or software; others call for a model that can interpret ambiguity or support a judgment. Janus is designed to coordinate those steps and change the underlying model where appropriate.

Cost and reliability advantages are goals we are measuring, not results we are claiming.

Make the handoffs clear

Know what came in, what happened, and what's allowed next.

A useful system needs to know what came in, what happened, what checks were made and whether the next step is allowed. Defined boundaries and records make it possible to review exceptions without asking a person to supervise every routine operation.

People retain the authority that matters

Employees delegate the task, not the decision.

The aim is not human approval of every minor step. It is to assign authority deliberately: automate what is already permitted, stop when evidence is insufficient, and seek authorized human judgment when the situation calls for it.

The review and escalation experience is still being developed and tested.

Meet Arbiter

The first Janus application.

Arbiter is the starting point for people who don't write automation code. It is designed to interview the work out of an expert, surface missing details and help shape a process Janus can coordinate.

Arbiter shows one way to use the OS; it is not the whole OS.

Where we are now

Tested on our own work first.

DTM is using an early implementation of these ideas in the development and testing of Janus itself. This internal work provides a real testbed for bounded tasks, checks, holds and review practices, while important pieces of the broader experience remain under development.

Janus is in private beta; this is not a claim of production maturity across customer workflows.
