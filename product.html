The problem

We built AI systems that mimic human communication and thought. Then we forgot to teach them the decision boxes we teach humans.

Organizations running AI workflows today have speed. What they don't have is memory, governance, or continuity. When something goes wrong there is no record of why it was approved, who decided, or when the drift started.

The models are not broken. The structure around them is missing.

Memory is retrieval, not storage — a larger context window is just a more expensive way to get lost.

Gates

Hard stops at defined danger points. Nothing irreversible happens without a human decision.

Gates are not suggestions. They are governance primitives injected at the points where risk, ambiguity, policy, cost, or irreversible action requires review.

When a gate fires, the workflow stops. The operator sees what triggered it, reviews the context, and decides. Janus records the decision and the reason — not just the outcome.

When gates fire
Risk. Ambiguity. Policy boundary. Cost threshold. Irreversible action. Any condition your organization defines as requiring human judgment.
What gates produce
A structured decision record. Who reviewed it, what they saw, what they decided, and why. Every override is audited. Every approval is linked to the plan it governed.
Gate contract
One page, plain language. What triggers the gate. What it evaluates. What it returns. What happens at each decision point. Defined by your organization before deployment.
Operator mental model
Three gates. Intent — approve. Plan — approve. Run — review — restart. Simple enough to hold in working memory. Complex enough to govern a production workflow.

Critical-thinking hygiene

The system challenges its own output before you approve it.

Hygiene is not a review step bolted on at the end. It runs continuously — on intent, on plans, on decisions, on memory — before anything reaches a human gate.

The goal is not perfection from the model. The goal is that every input that reaches an operator has been challenged, scored, and surfaced with its weaknesses visible.

Intent hygiene
Before a plan is generated, the intent is scored across structured bins. Ambiguous inputs are surfaced and resolved — not passed through.
Plan hygiene
The plan itself goes through the same scoring ritual before ratification. Weak bins are flagged. The operator approves a plan that has been challenged, not one that has been generated.
Memory hygiene
Stale, contradictory, or low-confidence memory is pruned before it can influence a live workflow. Context that reaches execution is clean context.
Drift detection
Janus tracks intent from the moment a workflow begins. When agent output drifts from the ratified plan, the system surfaces it before it ships.
